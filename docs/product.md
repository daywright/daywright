# Product: the first job is health

Daywright's first job is to get busy people who were once in shape back in shape,
without them having to think about it. The owner is user number one.

## Who it is for

The comeback: people who were fit once and lost it to travel, work, migration or
children. They know roughly what to do. What they lack is the time and attention
to plan it, track it and stick to it.

## The loop

1. **Sense.** Read what already exists: the phone and watch (steps, sleep, heart
   rate, workouts), the smart scale, the calendar, the kitchen device.
2. **Decide.** Plan today's food, training and sleep around the person's time,
   equipment, rules and goal.
3. **Tell.** Give short, plain orders on whatever device is near.
4. **Prove.** Show change with photos and numbers, then adjust the plan.

The person does not log and does not plan. They do what it says.

## Rules you say once

Anything said once is kept, visible and editable (ADR-0015):

- "Remember I only have Fridays to work out." One full-body Friday session plus
  daily walking. Weekly volume matters more than how many days you train
  ([Iversen et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/)).
- "Keep the food simple and consistent each day." A few meals rotate, the
  shopping list repeats, and confirming a meal is one word.
- "Forget the Friday rule." Gone, and the plan changes.

## Food: prescribe, don't track

Tracking apps make you log everything you ate. Daywright plans meals from food you
would buy anyway, so most days confirming is one word: "done". In the kitchen the
voice scale makes it hands-free and measured: say "one big tomato" with it on the
scale, and Daywright answers "Large tomato, 182 grams, 33 calories. Logged."
(USDA FoodData Central lists a large tomato at 182 g and 18 kcal per 100 g.)

## A day

| Time | What happens |
|------|--------------|
| 07:30 | Step on the scale. The e-ink board on the fridge shows today's plan. |
| 08:00 | Same breakfast as always. "Done." |
| 13:00 | Lunch from the plan. "Done." |
| 18:30 | Friday: 25 minutes, full body, at home. The watch records it. |
| 22:30 | "Screens off in 30 minutes. You slept 5 h 40 last night." |
| Sunday | The weekly review: waist, strength, what changes next week and why. |

## Measurements that mean something

Precision comes from method more than from any single device:

1. **The same conditions every time** (morning, before food) and trends instead of
   single readings.
2. **Several cheap signals combined:** scale, waist, photos, strength log.
3. **Calibration against a gold standard:** an occasional DEXA scan, so the daily
   numbers learn each person's offset.

Measurements that matter most and cost least: waist under half your height
([NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/news/articles/keep-the-size-of-your-waist-to-less-than-half-of-your-height-nice--recommends)),
daily steps up to about 8,000 to 10,000 for adults under 60
([Paluch et al. 2022](https://doaj.org/article/c3639c468b4348958202e52793ab979d)),
strength and cardio fitness. These are associations, not proof of cause.

## Guardrails

Wellness, not medical; 18 and over; warning signs stop the plan; no drug coaching;
body photos processed on the device; health data opt-in and never sold. The full
list is ADR-0017 in `docs/decisions.md`.
